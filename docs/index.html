Import — CSV file, or paste from Numbers / eBay

Headers are matched by name when present (Purchase Price, Sold For, Final value fee, …). Without headers, columns are read in the database sheet's own order — Sport → Cert No. → Purchase → Sold → Expenses — so a paste straight out of Numbers lines up; Profit and ROI are ignored on import since they're recalculated. eBay: Seller Hub → Orders → Download report gives a CSV that imports cleanly as Sales only; buys can be pasted from your purchase history.

Cells work like a spreadsheet. Click to select, drag or ⇧+click for a range, ⌘C / ⌘V / ⌘X to copy, paste, and cut blocks — a paste spills right and down from the selected cell and adds rows if it needs them. ⌘A selects everything, ⌫ clears the selection. Just start typing to overwrite a cell, or ↩ / double-click to edit it; ↩ commits and moves down, ⇥ moves right, esc reverts. Arrow keys move around, and the selection's total shows next to the toolbar.

Copying gives you tab-separated text, so a range drops straight into Numbers — and Numbers cells paste straight back in. Paste a whole 16-column sheet row at the Sport column and it's read in the sheet's own order; anything else lands exactly where you point it. Comma-separated text is a different thing — send that through the Import panel, which works out the delimiter for you.

Profit = Sold − Purchase − Shipping − Grading − Fees. ROI = Profit ÷ total cost. Both columns are calculated, not editable, and are skipped on paste. The ledger lives in docs/data/transactions.csv in this repo — that file is what visitors see. Your edits stay in this browser until you hit Save to GitHub, and Export CSV is always a local backup.
